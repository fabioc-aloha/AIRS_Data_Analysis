# AIRS Typology Intervention Guide

**Version**: 1.0.0
**Created**: 2025-12-23
**Purpose**: Evidence-based intervention strategies for each AI readiness typology

---

## Overview

This guide provides detailed, actionable interventions for each of the three AIRS typologies. Recommendations are prioritized based on the research finding that **Price Value (PV)**, **Hedonic Motivation (HM)**, and **Social Influence (SI)** are the only significant predictors of AI adoption intention.

### Key Research Insight

> Traditional technology adoption drivers (Performance Expectancy, Effort Expectancy, Facilitating Conditions, Habit) do NOT predict AI adoption. Instead, focus interventions on:
> - **PV (β=.505)**: Demonstrating clear ROI and value
> - **HM (β=.217)**: Making AI enjoyable and engaging
> - **SI (β=.136)**: Leveraging peer influence and social proof

## AI Skeptics (AIRS Score ≤20)

### Profile Summary

| Attribute | Finding |
|-----------|---------||
| Population | ~17% of workforce |
| AIRS Score Range | 8-20 |
| Behavioral Intention | Low (Mean BI ≈ 1.5-2.5) |
| Trust Level | Low (-1.49σ below mean) |
| AI Anxiety | High (+0.76σ above mean) |
| Conversion Difficulty | High |

### Root Cause Analysis

Skeptics typically exhibit:
- **Deep distrust** in AI reliability and accuracy
- **High anxiety** about AI replacing jobs or making errors
- **Low perceived value** — don't see how AI helps *them*
- **Negative past experiences** or exposure to AI failures
- **Lack of control** — feel AI is imposed on them

### Intervention Framework: TRUST

| Letter | Focus | Rationale |
|--------|-------|-----------|
| **T** | Transparency | Show how AI works, demystify the "black box" |
| **R** | Relevance | Connect AI to *their* specific pain points |
| **U** | User Control | Emphasize AI assists, doesn't replace |
| **S** | Social Proof | Share stories from trusted peers (not executives) |
| **T** | Time | Allow gradual adoption, no pressure |

### Detailed Interventions

#### 1. Address Trust Barriers (Primary Focus)

**Objective**: Reduce AI anxiety and build confidence in AI reliability

| Action | Implementation | Timeline |
|--------|---------------|----------|
| AI Transparency Sessions | Live demos showing how AI makes decisions | Week 1-2 |
| Error Rate Communication | Share actual accuracy statistics (not 100%) | Week 1 |
| Human Override Options | Ensure all AI suggestions can be ignored | Always |
| Success/Failure Examples | Show both to build realistic expectations | Week 2-3 |
| Q&A with AI Users | Peer-led discussions, not management-led | Week 3-4 |

**Sample Messaging**:
> "AI tools are assistants, not replacements. You're always in control. Here's exactly how this tool works, including when it gets things wrong..."

#### 2. Demonstrate Personal Value (PV Focus)

**Objective**: Help skeptics see tangible benefit to *their* specific work

| Action | Implementation | Timeline |
|--------|---------------|----------|
| Pain Point Mapping | Interview to identify frustrating tasks | Week 1 |
| Targeted Demo | Show AI solving *their* specific problem | Week 2 |
| Time Savings Calculator | Quantify potential time saved per week | Week 2 |
| "Try It Once" Challenge | Single, low-stakes task with AI assistance | Week 3 |
| Before/After Comparison | Document actual results (not promised) | Week 4 |

**Sample Script**:
> "You mentioned you spend 3 hours every Monday on [task]. Let me show you how AI could reduce that to 30 minutes. But I want you to see for yourself — try it on one task, and if it doesn't help, no pressure to continue."

#### 3. Leverage Peer Influence (SI Focus)

**Objective**: Use trusted colleagues (not management) to share experiences

| Action | Implementation | Timeline |
|--------|---------------|----------|
| Identify Peer Ambassadors | Select respected colleagues (not early adopters) | Week 1 |
| Peer Storytelling | Skeptic-turned-user shares journey | Week 2-3 |
| Small Group Discussions | 3-5 person conversations, not presentations | Week 3-4 |
| Anonymous Q&A | Address concerns without judgment | Ongoing |
| Optional Observation | Watch a peer use AI, no participation required | Week 4 |

**Key Principle**: Skeptics trust peers more than executives or IT. Use recently-converted moderate users, not enthusiasts.

#### 4. Gradual Exposure Path

**Objective**: Create low-risk, reversible adoption steps

| Week | Activity | Commitment Level |
|------|----------|------------------|
| 1 | Watch a 5-min demo video | Passive |
| 2 | Observe a peer using AI | Passive |
| 3 | Try AI on one non-critical task | Low |
| 4 | Use AI assistance for one workflow | Medium |
| 5+ | Optional expansion based on experience | Self-directed |

### Metrics to Track

| Metric | Target | Measurement |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Trust Score (TR) | +0.5 points in 30 days | Re-assessment |
| Single Task Trial | 80% completion rate | Tracking |
| Peer Session Attendance | 60% of skeptics | Attendance |
| Anxiety Reduction | -0.3σ | Survey |

### What NOT to Do with Skeptics

| ❌ Avoid | Why |
|----------|-----|
| Mandatory training | Increases resistance and anxiety |
| Feature overload | Overwhelming, confirms "it's too hard" |
| Executive mandates | Feels coercive, destroys trust |
| Comparison to enthusiasts | Creates shame, not motivation |
| Dismissing concerns | Validates their distrust |

## Moderate Users (AIRS Score 21-30)

### Profile Summary

| Attribute | Finding |
|-----------|---------||
| Population | ~67% of workforce (largest segment) |
| AIRS Score Range | 21-30 |
| Behavioral Intention | Moderate (Mean BI ≈ 2.5-4.0) |
| Trust Level | Neutral (+0.01σ) |
| AI Anxiety | Mild (+0.42σ) |
| Conversion Potential | High (swing voters) |

### Root Cause Analysis

Moderate users typically:
- **See some value** but need more convincing
- **Use AI selectively** for specific tasks only
- **Follow the crowd** — will adopt when peers do
- **Balance cost vs. benefit** in every decision
- **Need clear ROI** before expanding usage

### Intervention Framework: VALUE

| Letter | Focus | Rationale |
|--------|-------|-----------|
| **V** | Visible Wins | Showcase concrete success stories |
| **A** | Accessible Training | Make learning easy, not mandatory |
| **L** | Low Friction | Remove barriers in existing workflows |
| **U** | Use Cases | Provide specific, relevant applications |
| **E** | Encouragement | Celebrate progress, normalize learning curve |

### Detailed Interventions

#### 1. Demonstrate Clear ROI (PV Focus — Primary)

**Objective**: Convert "maybe" to "yes" with tangible value proof

| Action | Implementation | Timeline |
|--------|---------------|----------|
| ROI Calculator | Personalized time/money savings estimate | Week 1 |
| Case Study Library | 3-5 stories from similar roles | Week 1-2 |
| A/B Comparison | Same task with and without AI | Week 2 |
| 30-Day Challenge | Track actual time saved | Week 2-5 |
| Success Sharing | Platform to share personal wins | Ongoing |

**Sample ROI Conversation**:
> "Based on your role, users like you save an average of 4 hours per week using AI for [task]. Over a year, that's 200+ hours. Let's test this with your actual workload."

#### 2. Make AI Enjoyable (HM Focus)

**Objective**: Transform AI from "another tool" to "helpful companion"

| Action | Implementation | Timeline |
|--------|---------------|----------|
| Gamification | Points/badges for AI usage milestones | Ongoing |
| "AI Surprise" Feature | Weekly tip that delights users | Weekly |
| Creative Challenges | Fun, non-work AI experiments | Monthly |
| Quick Wins Gallery | Showcase clever/creative AI uses | Ongoing |
| Stress Reduction Framing | "AI handles the boring parts" | Messaging |

**Sample Gamification**:
| Badge | Criteria | Points |
|-------|----------|--------|
| First Steps | Complete first AI-assisted task | 10 |
| Time Saver | Save 1 hour in a week | 25 |
| Explorer | Try 3 different AI features | 50 |
| Efficiency Expert | Save 10 hours in a month | 100 |

#### 3. Leverage Social Dynamics (SI Focus)

**Objective**: Create "fear of missing out" and positive peer pressure

| Action | Implementation | Timeline |
|--------|---------------|----------|
| Adoption Dashboard | Show % of team using AI | Always visible |
| Peer Leaderboard | Friendly competition (opt-in) | Ongoing |
| Team Challenges | Group goals with rewards | Monthly |
| Success Spotlights | Feature users in newsletters | Weekly |
| Lunch & Learn | Peer-taught sessions | Bi-weekly |

**Sample Messaging**:
> "73% of your team is now using AI for [task]. Here's what they're discovering..."

#### 4. Remove Friction Points

**Objective**: Make adoption easier than not adopting

| Friction Point | Solution |
|----------------|----------|
| Multiple logins | SSO integration |
| Finding tools | Single AI hub/dashboard |
| Learning curve | Contextual tooltips, not manuals |
| Workflow disruption | AI embedded in existing tools |
| Forgotten usage | Gentle reminders and prompts |

#### 5. Expand Use Cases

**Objective**: Move from single-task to multi-task AI usage

| Week | Expansion Target |
|------|------------------|
| 1-2 | Master first use case |
| 3-4 | Add second use case (related) |
| 5-6 | Explore third use case (different area) |
| 7-8 | Identify personal workflow optimization |
| 9+ | Self-directed exploration |

### Metrics to Track

| Metric | Target | Measurement |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Use Case Expansion | +1 use case per month | Usage tracking |
| Weekly Active Usage | 4+ days per week | Analytics |
| AIRS Score Improvement | +3 points in 60 days | Re-assessment |
| Time Saved | 3+ hours per week | Self-report |
| Referral Rate | 40% recommend to peers | Survey |

### What NOT to Do with Moderate Users

| ❌ Avoid | Why |
|----------|-----|
| Assuming they're "sold" | They're still deciding |
| One-size-fits-all training | Need personalized use cases |
| Ignoring their concerns | Small barriers stop adoption |
| Celebrating too early | Progress can reverse |
| Mandatory advanced features | Let them expand naturally |

## AI Enthusiasts (AIRS Score >30)

### Profile Summary

| Attribute | Finding |
|-----------|---------||
| Population | ~16% of workforce |
| AIRS Score Range | 31-40 |
| Behavioral Intention | High (Mean BI ≈ 4.0-5.0) |
| Trust Level | High (+1.37σ above mean) |
| AI Anxiety | Low (-0.86σ below mean) |
| Conversion Need | None (already advocates) |

### Root Cause Analysis

Enthusiasts typically:
- **Already believe** in AI's value and potential
- **Actively seek** new AI tools and features
- **Influence others** through example and advocacy
- **Want advanced capabilities** beyond basics
- **Risk becoming frustrated** if held back

### Intervention Framework: LEAD

| Letter | Focus | Rationale |
|--------|-------|-----------|
| **L** | Leadership Roles | Channel energy into influence |
| **E** | Early Access | Reward with preview features |
| **A** | Advanced Training | Feed their desire for mastery |
| **D** | Development Input | Involve in product decisions |

### Detailed Interventions

#### 1. Create Leadership Opportunities

**Objective**: Transform enthusiasts into multipliers

| Role | Description | Impact |
|------|-------------|--------|
| AI Champion | Official recognition and responsibilities | High visibility |
| Peer Mentor | 1:1 support for moderate users | Direct influence |
| Training Co-facilitator | Assist in workshops | Credibility builder |
| Use Case Hunter | Document and share new applications | Content creation |
| Feedback Liaison | Channel between users and IT | Product improvement |

**Champion Program Structure**:
| Level | Requirements | Perks |
|-------|--------------|-------|
| Bronze | 3 months active usage | Badge, early access |
| Silver | + Mentor 2 colleagues | Advanced training, swag |
| Gold | + Create 1 training resource | Product input, conference attendance |
| Platinum | + Lead workshop | Executive recognition, career credit |

#### 2. Provide Advanced Capabilities

**Objective**: Keep enthusiasts engaged and growing

| Offering | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Beta Access | Preview new features before release |
| Advanced Training | Power user courses, certifications |
| API Access | For technical enthusiasts |
| Custom Workflows | Build personalized AI automations |
| Innovation Lab | Sandbox for experimentation |

#### 3. Capture and Share Their Knowledge

**Objective**: Scale enthusiast insights to the organization

| Method | Format | Frequency |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Success Stories | Written case studies | Monthly |
| Video Tutorials | Screen recordings | As created |
| Live Demos | Lunch & learn sessions | Bi-weekly |
| Tips & Tricks | Slack/Teams channel | Ongoing |
| Office Hours | Drop-in Q&A | Weekly |

#### 4. Involve in Product Decisions

**Objective**: Make enthusiasts feel ownership and investment

| Involvement | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| Feature Requests | Prioritized review process |
| Usability Testing | Early feedback on new features |
| Advisory Board | Quarterly product direction input |
| Bug Reporting | Direct channel to product team |
| Roadmap Preview | Early visibility into plans |

### Metrics to Track

| Metric | Target | Measurement |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Colleagues Mentored | 3+ per quarter | Tracking |
| Content Created | 2+ pieces per quarter | Submissions |
| Session Attendance (as leader) | 80% of scheduled | Attendance |
| Influence Reach | 10+ people impacted | Survey |
| Satisfaction Score | 9+ out of 10 | Survey |

### What NOT to Do with Enthusiasts

| ❌ Avoid | Why |
|----------|-----|
| Basic training | Wastes their time, shows you don't understand them |
| Ignoring their ideas | Causes disengagement and frustration |
| Restricting access | Demotivates and may cause shadow IT |
| Taking them for granted | They can influence negatively if frustrated |
| No recognition | Enthusiasm fades without acknowledgment |

### Risk: Enthusiast Burnout

Watch for signs of burnout from over-reliance:
- Constantly asked to help others
- Expected to lead every session
- No time for their own work
- Feeling uncompensated for extra effort

**Mitigation**: Rotate responsibilities, provide recognition, consider formal role adjustments.

## Cross-Typology Strategies

### Organization-Wide Initiatives

| Initiative | Skeptics | Moderate | Enthusiasts |
|------------|----------|----------|-------------|
| AI Town Hall | Optional attendance | Encouraged | Present/lead |
| Success Stories | Receive as reassurance | Receive and contribute | Primary contributors |
| Training Budget | Beginner courses | Intermediate + certification | Advanced + conferences |
| Tool Access | Basic tools only | Full standard access | Beta + experimental |
| Support Channel | High-touch human support | Self-service + human | Peer community + escalation |

### Manager Talking Points by Typology

**For Skeptics**:
> "I understand you have concerns about AI. Let's talk about them. There's no pressure to adopt anything immediately. What would need to be true for you to try a single, small experiment?"

**For Moderate Users**:
> "You've been using AI for [task] — how's that going? I'm curious if there are other areas where you think it might help. What's stopping you from trying more?"

**For Enthusiasts**:
> "You're clearly ahead of the curve on AI. How can we channel your knowledge to help the team? What advanced capabilities would make you even more effective?"

### Intervention Timing

| Trigger | Skeptic Action | Moderate Action | Enthusiast Action |
|---------|----------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| New AI Tool Launch | Delay introduction, prep trust content | Include in rollout with use cases | Early access, feedback collection |
| Performance Review | Do not tie to AI usage | Mention AI as optional skill | Recognize AI leadership |
| Team Meeting | Normalize AI discussion | Share peer success stories | Invite to demo |
| Onboarding | Brief intro, no pressure | Standard AI training | Identify and fast-track |

## Measurement & Iteration

### Re-Assessment Cadence

| Typology | Re-assess Interval | Trigger for Earlier Re-assessment |
|----------|-------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Skeptics | 90 days | Completed intervention program |
| Moderate | 60 days | Significant usage change |
| Enthusiasts | 120 days | Role change or new tools |

### Success Metrics by Typology

| Metric | Skeptic Target | Moderate Target | Enthusiast Target |
|--------|---------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| AIRS Score Change | +5 points | +3 points | Maintain |
| Weekly AI Usage | 1+ day | 4+ days | Daily |
| Typology Progression | Move to Moderate | Move to Enthusiast | Stay Enthusiast |
| Net Promoter Score | Neutral (0) | Positive (+30) | Very Positive (+60) |

### Continuous Improvement

After each intervention cycle:
1. **Analyze** which interventions moved scores
2. **Interview** typology movers (what worked?)
3. **Adjust** intervention mix based on data
4. **Share** findings with leadership
5. **Iterate** on intervention design
